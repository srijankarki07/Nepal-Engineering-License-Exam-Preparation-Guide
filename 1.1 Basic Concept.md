# 1.1 BASIC CONCEPTS

---

### 1. Ohm’s Law

**Definition:**
At constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage (V) across it.

V = I \* R

where,

- (V) = voltage (in volts),
- (I) = current (in amperes),
- (R) = resistance (in ohms, Ω)

**Key Point:** Ohm’s law is valid only for _linear_, _ohmic_ conductors (where resistance remains constant).

**Not applicable for:** Diodes, transistors, vacuum tubes — because their V–I relationship is non-linear.

**MCQ Tip:**

> “Which of the following is non-ohmic?” <br> Answer: _Diode, filament lamp, transistor._

### 2. Electric Voltage, Current, Power, and Energy

#### (a) Electric Current (I)

Flow of electric charge per unit time.
<br>
I = Q/t
<br>

Unit: **Ampere (A)**

1 A = 1 Coulomb / second

---

#### (b) Voltage (V)

Work done to move a unit charge between two points.
<br>

V = W/Q

<br>

Unit: **Volt (V)**

---

#### (c) Electric Power (P)

Rate at which electrical energy is converted into another form.
<br>

P = V \* I
<br>

Also:
</br>

![alt text](/assets/image.png)

Unit: **Watt (W)**

---

#### (d) Electrical Energy (E)

E = P \* t

Unit: **Joule (J)**<br>
Commercial unit: **kWh (kilowatt-hour)**
<br>1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

**MCQ Example:**

> A bulb rated 100 W, 230 V runs for 5 hours. Find energy consumed.
> (E = 100 × 5 = 500 Wh = 0.5 kWh)

### 3. Conducting and Insulating Materials

| Property      | Conductors               | Insulators        |
| ------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Resistivity   | Low                      | High              |
| Example       | Copper, Aluminum         | Rubber, Glass     |
| Electron flow | Free electrons available | No free electrons |
| Application   | Wires, Busbars           | Cable insulation  |

**Semiconductors** lie in between (e.g., Silicon, Germanium).

### 7. Linear and Non-linear Circuits

| Type       | Definition                                     | Example           |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Linear     | V–I relationship is straight line (constant R) | Resistor          |
| Non-linear | V–I curve not straight                         | Diode, transistor |

---

### 8. Bilateral and Unilateral Circuits

| Type       | Meaning                                          | Example             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| Bilateral  | Same current–voltage relation in both directions | Resistor, capacitor |
| Unilateral | Behavior changes with current direction          | Diode, transistor   |

---

### 9. Active and Passive Circuits

| Type    | Function                             | Example                       |
| ------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Active  | Can amplify or generate energy       | Transistor, OP-AMP            |
| Passive | Cannot generate energy; only consume | Resistor, capacitor, inductor |

**MCQ Example:**

> Which is active? → _Transistor_
> Which is passive? → _Inductor_

---

 **Summary Quick Review:**

- Ohm’s law → (V = IR)
- (P = VI = I^2R = V^2/R)
- KCL → charge; KVL → energy
- Star ↔ Delta conversions
- Linear ≠ Non-linear; Bilateral ≠ Unilateral
- Active ≠ Passive
