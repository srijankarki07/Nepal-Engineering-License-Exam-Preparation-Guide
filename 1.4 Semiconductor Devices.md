# 1.4 SEMICONDUCTOR DEVICES

This section focuses on **diodes, BJTs, MOSFETs, and CMOS** — all of which are _fundamental components_ in electronics.

## 1. Semiconductor Basics

### (a) Definition

A **semiconductor** is a material whose **electrical conductivity lies between** that of a conductor and an insulator.
Examples: **Silicon (Si)** and **Germanium (Ge)**

At 0 K → behaves as insulator.<br>
At room temperature → behaves as a conductor.

---

### (b) Types of Semiconductors

| Type       | Doping Material                         | Majority Carrier | Minority Carrier |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| **n-type** | Pentavalent (e.g., Phosphorus, Arsenic) | Electrons        | Holes            |
| **p-type** | Trivalent (e.g., Boron, Gallium)        | Holes            | Electrons        |

**Mnemonic:**<br>
p-type → _positive_ holes dominate.<br>
n-type → _negative_ electrons dominate.

---

## 2. Semiconductor Diode

A **diode** is a device formed by joining a **p-type** and **n-type** semiconductor → called **p-n junction**.<br>
It allows **current in one direction only.**

**Symbol:**
➡️ (Triangle pointing to a line)

---

### (a) Biasing of Diode

#### 1️⃣ Forward Bias

- p-side connected to **positive** terminal of battery
- n-side connected to **negative** terminal<br>
  → Junction barrier reduces → current flows.

**Characteristic:** Low resistance path.

#### 2️⃣ Reverse Bias

- p-side connected to **negative** terminal
- n-side connected to **positive** terminal<br>
  → Junction barrier widens → negligible current.

**Characteristic:** High resistance path (only small leakage current).

---

### (b) V–I Characteristics of Diode

- Forward region: exponential rise after threshold voltage

  - For **Si diode:** ≈ 0.7 V
  - For **Ge diode:** ≈ 0.3 V

- Reverse region: almost zero current until **breakdown voltage (Vz)**.

---

### (c) Applications of Diode

- Rectifier (AC → DC conversion)
- Clipper and Clamper circuits
- Voltage regulation (Zener diode)
- Signal demodulation

**MCQ Examples:**

> The forward voltage drop of a silicon diode is about **0.7 V** <br>
> A diode allows current to flow in **one direction only** <br>

---

## 3. Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)

A **BJT** consists of **two p-n junctions** and three terminals:

- **Emitter (E)**
- **Base (B)**
- **Collector (C)**

Types:

1. **NPN**
2. **PNP**

**Symbol:**

- Arrow **points out** for NPN
- Arrow **points in** for PNP<br>
  (Arrow always shows conventional current direction)

---

### (a) Working Principle

A transistor acts as a **current-controlled device** —
A small base current controls a large collector current.

I<sub>c</sub> = βI<sub>B</sub><br>

where β = current gain (typically 20–200).

---

### (b) Configurations

| Configuration             | Input | Output | Current Gain | Application                 |
| ------------------------- | ----- | ------ | ------------ | --------------------------- |
| **Common Base (CB)**      | E–B   | C–B    | <1           | High voltage gain           |
| **Common Emitter (CE)**   | B–E   | C–E    | 20–200       | Amplifier                   |
| **Common Collector (CC)** | B–C   | E–C    | >1           | Buffer / Impedance matching |

**MCQ Tip:**

> In common-emitter configuration, the transistor gives **both voltage and current gain.**

---

### (c) Biasing of BJT

To make transistor active:

- **Emitter-Base Junction (EBJ)** → **Forward biased**
- **Collector-Base Junction (CBJ)** → **Reverse biased**

---

### (d) Regions of Operation

| Region     | EB Junction | CB Junction | Function         |
| ---------- | ----------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| Cutoff     | Reverse     | Reverse     | Transistor OFF   |
| Active     | Forward     | Reverse     | Amplifier region |
| Saturation | Forward     | Forward     | Switch ON fully  |

---

### (e) Small-Signal and Large-Signal Models

- **Small-signal model:** used for amplifiers (linear region).
- **Large-signal model:** used for switching (ON/OFF).

---

## 4. MOSFET (Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor)

A **voltage-controlled device** — input voltage controls current flow between **source** and **drain**.
Used widely in ICs and microprocessors.

**Terminals:**

- Gate (G)
- Drain (D)
- Source (S)

---

### (a) Types of MOSFETs

| Type                 | Subtype               | Channel Formed by                             | Control                       |
| -------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Depletion type**   | n-channel / p-channel | Channel exists at zero bias                   | Depletion reduces current     |
| **Enhancement type** | n-channel / p-channel | Channel formed only when gate voltage applied | Enhancement increases current |

**Common in modern circuits:** **n-channel enhancement MOSFET**

---

### (b) Basic Working (n-channel Enhancement)

- When V<sub>GS</sub> < V <sub>th</sub> : on conduction (OFF).
- When V<sub>GS</sub> ≥ V <sub>th</sub> : channel forms → current flows (ON).

**High input impedance** (because of oxide layer).

---

**MCQ Examples:**

> MOSFET is a **voltage-controlled device** <br>
> BJT is a **current-controlled device** <br>
> n-channel MOSFET conducts when V<sub>GS</sub> ≥ V <sub>th

---

## 5. CMOS (Complementary MOS)

**CMOS = Complementary Metal-Oxide Semiconductor**

It uses **both n-channel and p-channel MOSFETs** in one circuit usually one turns ON when the other turns OFF.

### (a) Advantages

- **Very low power consumption**
- **High noise immunity**
- Used in **digital logic ICs**, **microprocessors**, and **memory devices**

### (b) Logic Behavior (Simplified)

| Input | n-MOS | p-MOS | Output |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ------ |
| 0     | OFF   | ON    | 1      |
| 1     | ON    | OFF   | 0      |

Hence, a **CMOS inverter** forms the basic logic NOT gate.

---

**Quick Summary Table**

| Device     | Control Type          | Major Terminals | Function         | Key Property          |
| ---------- | --------------------- | --------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- |
| **Diode**  | N/A                   | Anode, Cathode  | Rectifier        | Conducts one way      |
| **BJT**    | Current-controlled    | E, B, C         | Amplifier/Switch | Low input impedance   |
| **MOSFET** | Voltage-controlled    | G, D, S         | Switch/Amplifier | High input impedance  |
| **CMOS**   | Complementary MOSFETs | —               | Logic circuits   | Low power dissipation |

---

**Common Exam Pointers:**

- Si diode threshold: 0.7 V; Ge: 0.3 V
- In CE mode → high voltage & current gain
- MOSFET → insulated gate, high input resistance
- CMOS → complementary pair (n & p type), used in ICs
- BJT: current-controlled; FET/MOSFET: voltage-controlled
