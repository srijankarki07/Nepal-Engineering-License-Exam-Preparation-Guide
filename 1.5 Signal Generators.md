# 1.5 Signal Generators

### What is a Signal Generator?

A **signal generator** is an electronic device (or circuit) that produces **electrical waveforms** (signals) over a wide range of frequencies.
These signals are used to **test**, **analyze**, or **stimulate** electronic circuits.

In short: It **creates AC signals** (sine, square, triangular, etc.) of desired frequency and amplitude.

---

### Classification of Signal Generators

1. **Sine wave generator** – produces sinusoidal waveform
2. **Square/Triangular wave generator** – produces square or triangle waves
3. **Pulse generator** – produces pulses of adjustable width and amplitude
4. **Function generator** – can generate multiple waveforms (sine, square, triangle, ramp)

---

## Basic Principle of Waveform Generation

Every signal generator or oscillator works on **positive feedback**:

> “Part of the output signal is fed back to the input **in phase** with the original signal.”

If the feedback satisfies certain conditions (Barkhausen Criterion), **continuous oscillations** occur without external input.

### Barkhausen Criterion:

For sustained oscillations:

1. **Loop gain = 1 (|Aβ| = 1)**
2. **Phase shift = 0° or 360°** (feedback is positive)

Where:

- A = amplifier gain
- β = feedback factor

## Types of Oscillators

### 1. **RC Oscillator**

- Uses **resistor (R)** and **capacitor (C)** network for phase shift.
- Best for **low-frequency (audio range)** signals (below 1 MHz).
- Common types:

  - **RC Phase Shift Oscillator**
  - **Wien Bridge Oscillator**

#### ➤ RC Phase Shift Oscillator:

- Has 3 RC sections giving 180° phase shift.
- Transistor or op-amp provides another 180°, total = 360° (or 0°).
- Produces **sine wave**.

#### ➤ Wien Bridge Oscillator:

- Uses a bridge circuit of resistors and capacitors.
- Produces **very stable and distortion-free sine waves**.
- Commonly used in **audio frequency generators**.

### 2. **LC Oscillator**

- Uses **inductor (L)** and **capacitor (C)** network.
- Best for **high-frequency (RF range)** oscillations (above 1 MHz).

#### Common Types:

- **Hartley Oscillator** → two inductors and one capacitor
- **Colpitts Oscillator** → one inductor and two capacitors

#### ➤ Frequency of LC Oscillator:

![alt text](/assets/image-16.png)

#### 💡 Key difference:

- LC → High frequency
- RC → Low frequency

---

### 3 **Crystal Oscillator**

- Uses a **quartz crystal** for frequency control.
- Works on **piezoelectric effect** — crystal vibrates at a natural frequency when voltage is applied.
- **Highly stable** and **precise** frequency.
- Used in **clocks, microcontrollers, computers, transmitters, etc.**

## Quick Exam Pointers:

- **RC → low frequency**, **LC → high frequency**, **Crystal → very stable**
- **Wien bridge** uses **RC network** and **op-amp**
- **Piezoelectric effect** → converts electrical energy ↔ mechanical vibrations
- **Positive feedback** is essential for oscillation
- **Barkhausen criterion** must be satisfied
- **No external input** needed for oscillators
